# Genome-based prediction of drug susceptibility, minimum inhibitory concentration, and pre-resistance risk in *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*: a reproducible analysis of 12,245 clinical isolates

*Structured abstract*

**Background.** Phenotypic drug-susceptibility testing (DST) of *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* requires weeks of culture, delaying effective therapy; in Thailand the reported median interval from specimen to DST result is 114 days. Whole-genome sequencing can infer resistance within hours, and catalogue-based tools are already deployed, but most operate on binary susceptibility and do not quantify the risk that a currently-treatable isolate will acquire resistance.

**Methods.** Using the CRyPTIC compendium (n = 12,245 isolates with matched genome and 96-well broth-microdilution phenotypes), we translated each isolate's variant calls to amino-acid substitutions, restricted features to World Health Organization (WHO) catalogue resistance loci, and trained, with 30% held-out evaluation, (i) per-drug susceptibility classifiers, (ii) regression models for minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC), and (iii) a pre-resistance analysis in which a drug's own target gene was withheld. A within-host analysis used the 226 patients contributing serial isolates, with patient-grouped cross-validation and a bootstrap confidence interval.

**Results.** Susceptibility prediction across nine drugs reached a mean AUROC of 0.94 (specificity 97%). MIC was predicted to within one doubling dilution (essential agreement) in 79% of held-out isolates. With the target gene removed, genomic background anticipated second-line resistance at AUROC 0.83–0.94 and retained discrimination among catalogue-susceptible isolates (moxifloxacin AUROC 0.71). Compensatory *rpoC* mutations increased monotonically with resistance breadth (0.32 in pan-susceptible to 0.64 in pre-extensively-resistant isolates). In the within-host analysis, a susceptible isolate's background predicted subsequent resistance with an AUROC of 0.66 (95% CI 0.60–0.73), lower than the cross-sectional estimate as expected for a prospective task; specific evolvability markers showed no individual enrichment.

**Conclusions.** An interpretable, fully open pipeline reproduces deployed susceptibility prediction, extends it to quantitative MIC, and characterises a pre-resistance signal on the complete cohort that a within-host analysis confirms is genuine but modest. Fully dated, prospective forecasting by longitudinal survival analysis on time-stamped isolates remains the principal next step. The work is positioned as decision support and genomic surveillance for high-burden settings and is intended to augment, not replace, confirmatory DST.
