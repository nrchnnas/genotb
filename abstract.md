# Genome-based prediction of drug susceptibility, minimum inhibitory concentration, and pre-resistance risk in *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*: a reproducible analysis of 12,245 clinical isolates

*Structured abstract*

**Background.** Phenotypic drug-susceptibility testing (DST) of *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* requires weeks of culture, delaying effective therapy; in Thailand the reported median interval from specimen to DST result is 114 days. Whole-genome sequencing can infer resistance within hours, and catalogue-based tools are already deployed, but most operate on binary susceptibility and do not quantify the risk that a currently-treatable isolate will acquire resistance.

**Methods.** Using the CRyPTIC compendium (n = 12,245 isolates with matched genome and 96-well broth-microdilution phenotypes), we translated each isolate's variant calls to amino-acid substitutions, restricted features to World Health Organization (WHO) catalogue resistance loci, and trained, with 30% held-out evaluation, (i) per-drug susceptibility classifiers, (ii) regression models for minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC), and (iii) a pre-resistance analysis in which a drug's own target gene was withheld. A within-host analysis used the 226 patients contributing serial isolates, with patient-grouped cross-validation and a bootstrap confidence interval.

**Results.** Susceptibility prediction across nine drugs reached a mean AUROC of 0.94 (specificity 97%). MIC was predicted to within one doubling dilution (essential agreement) in 79% of held-out isolates. With the target gene removed, genomic background predicted second-line resistance at AUROC 0.83–0.94, but a specificity analysis attributes most of this to multidrug-resistant (MDR) status rather than to pre-resistance: a single binary MDR indicator alone reached AUROC 0.813 versus 0.894 for the full 2,233-feature background model (gain +0.081, 95% CI 0.070–0.092), and discrimination fell to 0.725 within non-MDR isolates. Background features nonetheless retained discrimination among catalogue-susceptible isolates (moxifloxacin AUROC 0.71). In the within-host analysis of 226 patients with serial isolates, a susceptible isolate's background predicted subsequent resistance with an AUROC of 0.66 (95% CI 0.60–0.73); specific evolvability markers showed no individual enrichment.

**Conclusions.** An interpretable, fully open pipeline reproduces deployed susceptibility prediction and extends it to quantitative MIC. The background "pre-resistance" signal is shown to be largely confounded by MDR status, and we report the specificity analysis that establishes this; any such claim requires the same control. Fully dated, prospective forecasting by longitudinal survival analysis on time-stamped isolates remains the principal next step. The work is positioned as decision support and genomic surveillance for high-burden settings and is intended to augment, not replace, confirmatory DST.
